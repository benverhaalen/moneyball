# Art of the Deal's trade evaluation and deal curation

The product evaluates a complete decision, not the sum of the incoming names. Preserve the process already implemented in the shared service and trade/waiver accounting.

## Define and validate the branches

Resolve actual identities, owners, future rights and the precise package. Separate an existing offer, a hypothetical proposal, and each counteroffer. Require explicit drops, roster placements and an evaluation week. Reject infeasible ownership or capacity branches before discussing value. Unverified lock/review/IR/taxi semantics remain operational gaps even when the tool's modeled roster is valid.

Compare **hold plus feasible management** with **trade plus feasible management**, under the same cutoff, source-coherent football assumptions, horizon and future-management standard. Include a named current waiver alternative and doing nothing when relevant. Never give only the trade branch a guaranteed future rescue or resale.

## Trace the actual service

Solve both teams' full legal before/after lineups, including FLEX cascades. Read `new_starters`, `displaced_starters`, and `still_scores_but_moves_slot`: someone who scores on both branches cancels even when their slot changes. Compare incoming assets with the feasible incumbents they actually displace, not with zero or the outgoing player in isolation.

Charge lost bench coverage, necessary drops, roster space, byes, IR/taxi returns and acquisition resources. Use named absence and role-change scenarios and identify the best attainable recovery. A zero immediate starter gain may still buy coverage, but it needs a deployment state and opportunity cost.

Trace availability → NFL role → opportunity → conversion → legal starts → competitive benefit. Distinguish injury incidence, absence duration, participation and restored role. Do not discount medical risk twice if it is already embedded in a projection. xFP describes received opportunity; a production gap alone is not future usage evidence.

Apply the actual service window: transaction review, game locks, waiver processing, information arrival, trade deadline, qualification and fantasy final. Pure redraft has no post-final player service. Keeper rights require eligibility, cost and displaced alternatives. Dynasty future service and picks need explicit scenarios and conservation; age alone supplies neither a premium nor a price.

## Curate deals that have a reason to happen

Find counterparties whose actual lineup needs and replacements differ from yours. Construct a bounded set of legal packages and inspect each with `trade_packet`. Explain why the other manager could prefer each and the preference or fact that would make them decline. Their improvement alone does not defeat a mutually useful exchange; competitive position can still matter.

Use package menus to probe different compensation preferences when useful, not as an automatic concession ladder. Do not infer private valuations or acceptance probabilities from a roster, one rejection or generic positional needs. No tool here negotiates, sends messages or submits trades.

## Research and report the deciding uncertainty

Read compact `player_evidence`, expand only relevant players, and research missing links that could reverse the ordering. Refresh official status/role and coherent forecasts; archived dossiers are not current injury reports. Props are dated thresholds, not means or ROS projections; multiple books are not independent measurements.

Return accept, decline, counter, or wait for a named fact, with confidence stated separately. Follow the packet's `decision_support.answer_contract`: explain who benefits (you, them, both, neither, roughly even, or uncertain), using an explicit criterion and horizon. A trade need not have one loser; distinguish who gains more from whether accepting helps this user's team.

When the trade is relatively even, explain what tips the decision and why: usable weeks, named coverage, timing, retained flexibility, a verified outside option, or the manager's objective. Show what is given up to get that advantage. A point gap is not automatically meaningful; no universal near-even tolerance or confidence follows from these forecasts. If plausible uncertainty reverses the ordering, make that visible and justify holding, waiting or countering instead of inventing a winner. Missing data does not mean the trade is even.

Explain actual substitutions, coverage cost, outside options, the strongest countercase and reversal conditions. Provider-baseline point differences are diagnostics. Title probabilities require calibrated joint forecasts and exact competition mechanics; a simulation or stored packet alone does not supply them.

Expose the projection that controls the choice: "If you expect X to provide more usable service than Y over this horizon, and the other package costs are covered, accept; otherwise hold or counter." Choose the actions from the actual branches, rather than always treating an incoming player winning this comparison as sufficient. Name the horizon, actual displaced starter, projection source, other necessary assumptions and uncertainty. Separate your current recommendation from the user's alternative projection. Where coherent inputs permit it, calculate the break-even; otherwise describe the switching condition and evidence needed without inventing a number.
